How AQI is Measured

Pollutant Monitoring

AQI tracks pollutants like PM2.5, PM10, CO, SO₂, NO₂, and ozone using air quality monitoring stations.

Data Collection

Sensors measure pollutant concentrations in real-time, often hourly, at urban and rural sites.

AQI Calculation

Each pollutant’s concentration is converted to a sub-index using EPA standards, with the highest sub-index becoming the AQI.

Global standards, like those from the EPA, ensure consistent AQI reporting worldwide.

AQI Levels and Health Impacts

Good (0-50)

Air quality is satisfactory, posing little or no risk to health.

Moderate (51-100)

Acceptable air quality, but sensitive groups may experience minor effects.

Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (101-150)

Sensitive groups (e.g., elderly, children) may experience health effects.

Unhealthy (151-200)

Everyone may experience health effects; sensitive groups face more serious issues.

Very Unhealthy (201-300)

Health alert: everyone may experience serious health effects.

Hazardous (301-500)

Emergency conditions: the entire population is likely to be affected.
